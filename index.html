Dig It Right: Bringing Real-World Archaeology to the Public

  • Our project is an archaeology-themed Roblox game called “Dig It Right!”
  • Through this project, we had the chance not only to play games, but also to build and code our own.
  • We discussed archaeology and our game idea with Dr. Barbara Silber from West Chester University and learned a lot about real archaeology.
  • We received valuable feedback for improving the game, such as adding a lab mode.
  • We shared our game demo with other kids, and they said they learned new facts about real archaeology.

• Game Development Overview

This section describes how our archaeology game was researched, designed, built, and improved through feedback and iteration.
  • Focused on realism, engagement, and education
  • Developed through team collaboration and iteration
  • Refined using expert feedback from archaeologists

• Research & Inspiration

We began by studying existing archaeology-related games and educational resources.
  • Most existing games focus on combat or treasure hunting
  • Very few represent real archaeological practice
  • Tools of the Trade of the American Museum of Natural History from Science Trek was identified as a strong educational reference
  • Our goal was to build something more engaging and more accurate

• Decision, Concept, and Prototype

The team discussed how to turn our educational idea into a fun and accessible game.
  • Unlike most archaeology-themed games: unrealistic, inaccurate, goal-misrepresenting, and educationally weak.
  • Different from rare educational games that are informative but too simple to stay engaging.
  • We decided to expand the idea into a more immersive Roblox experience.
  • The team agreed on Dig It Right!, a fun, realistic, and educational game using real tools, processes, and roles, with careful excavation, data recording, and museum building.

• Virtual Dig Site: First Implementation

We designed three main roles to represent real archaeological work.
  • Excavator: The primary role in this version, responsible for digging.
  • Analyst: Provides GPS to track locations and glue to piece artifacts together.
  • Recorder: Accompanies the excavator, documenting discoveries with a notebook and camera.

• Design Integration

Archaeological tools and actions were integrated directly into gameplay.
  • Tools were mapped to real excavation steps
  • Replace drones and ground penetrating radar with magnetometer for easier implementation
  • New mechanics are introduced as players dig deeper
  • Gameplay follows a realistic excavation workflow

• Planning & Task Distribution

The game plan developed naturally as the team defined assets and features.
  • Dozens of items and assets were listed
  • Team members selected items based on interest
  • The remaining tasks were distributed evenly
  • This ensured a balanced workload across the team

• Development Workflow

The team collaborated using shared tools and regular meetings.
  • Models were developed individually in a shared project
  • The team met every Tuesday and Thursday
  • Roblox Live Collaborators was used to merge work
  • Frequent integration and testing improved stability

• Version Control & Iteration

Version control supported safe experimentation and continuous improvement.
  • Allowed rollback to earlier builds when issues occurred
  • Hundreds of versions were created during development
  • Each version represented an iteration or improvement

• Expert Review

The first playable version was reviewed by archaeology experts.
  • Presented to Dr. Giovine and Prof. Silber
  • Location: West Chester University Museum of Anthropology and Archaeology
  • Feedback was based on real-world archaeology practices
  • Confirmed overall accuracy of the game’s approach

• Key Feedback

The experts suggested several improvements to increase realism and clarity.
  • Add layered soil strata with different colors
  • Distribute artifacts from newer to older layers with depth
  • Use a more flexible grid system based on artifact density
  • Add protective gear and water for excavators
  • Add NPCs or hints to guide players and highlight the dig site

• Final Result

After implementing the feedback, the game improved significantly.
  • More realistic excavation experience
  • More engaging and educational gameplay
  • Better guidance and environmental storytelling
  • Clear improvement over the initial prototype

• The clues for spotting errors in images and news

Archaeology vs. Paleontology
  • It’s generally incorrect to categorize dinosaur fossils as archaeology.
  • Dinosaur fossils belong to Paleontology, the study of ancient non-human life.
  • Human artifacts and human remains belong to Archaeology.
  • Both fields use similar excavation techniques and study the past, which often leads to public confusion.
  • There is occasional overlap, especially with ancient human remains (prehistoric archaeology).
  • Because dinosaur fossils are not part of human culture, they are paleontological, not archaeological—even though both fields study old things from the ground.
Archaeologists: Study the past scientifically to understand human history.
  • Focus: Emphasize context, preservation, and sharing knowledge with the public.
  • Methods: Use meticulous techniques, such as forensic analysis.
Treasure Hunters: Seek valuable items for personal profit rather than research.
  • Impact: Often destroy historical context by selling artifacts, treating sites as commercial resources.
  • Consequence: Deprives researchers and the public of a comprehensive understanding of historical records.

Our Road to Robot Success!

  • Our game strategy is unique because of our smart use of the color sensor and custom block coding.
  • We use the color sensor to switch each run’s program, which helps us save time during matches.
  • Our block coding allows us to program movements and actions with high precision.
  • We use a universal baseplate docking system for all attachments, making installation fast and easy.
  • Each attachment went through at least three iterations to ensure reliable performance.
  • Our team was recognized with the Robot Game Award in both the scrimmage and the qualifier.
